# 2026-10-23: The 3D cutscene Lance, and the cast's portraits made consistent

**Who:** Mclaren, with Claude Code.

## What was asked

- Make the three draft expression sheets (Lance, Pat, Ysha) consistent: the same pixel
  size, colours and lines.
- Then, to use less AI-generated art in cutscenes: make the characters who matter in
  cutscenes full 3D models, like Bokura no Kazoku, but still in the pixel art style.
  Start with Lance, low poly, looking exactly like his expression sheet.

## Decisions

- **Cutscene characters are low-poly 3D models drawn like the portraits.** The world is
  already 3D rendered as pixel art, so a model in it gets whole pixels for free. Each part
  is exactly two tones from the portraits' palette (`toon.gdshader`), the model has a
  one-pixel black line (`outline.gdshader`), and the face is a pixel texture of the
  sheet's eight expressions, swapped like portraits. The model is the character, so he
  cannot drift the way generated pictures do.
- **The model is built by a Blender script** (`tools/blender/lance.py`), not by hand,
  so it can be rebuilt and changed in small steps; the `.blend` it saves
  (`assets/models/lance.blend`) is there to open and edit. The `.blend` stays outside the
  Godot project so Godot never tries to import it with Blender.
- **The outline is per model** (the one exception to "edges.gdshader handles every line"):
  an inverted hull pushed out by whole screen pixels, set back 3.5 cm from the camera so
  touching locks of hair draw no line, only real steps in depth.
- **Skin and face shade later** (threshold -0.35), so his face stays lit but for its
  edge, like an anime face; a hard shadow split down the middle looked wrong.
- **The portraits were rebuilt to one set of rules, not regenerated**, since generating
  again brings the drift back: `assets/drafts/characters/portraits/` samples each sheet
  onto one 64 x 82 grid, maps it to one palette (shared lines, eyes and shirt; two tones a
  part) and cleans it.

## What changed

- `tools/blender/lance.py` (the model, about 3,200 triangles: a round, messy mop of short,
  wide locks with a few wisps and bangs to the brows, the collar, tie, straps and
  backpack, black trousers), `tools/blender/preview.py` (quick looks from four sides).
- `src/drafts/aps-godot/actor.gd`: loads a model, gives each part (by material name) its
  palette, shading and line; `set_expression()`; `--actor-capture=<dir>` renders the cast
  alone at the portraits' density (136 px a metre). `aps.gd`: `--lance` (and
  `--face=<expression>`) stands him beside the student; the `actor_lance` check.
- `shaders/toon.gdshader`, `shaders/outline.gdshader`, `models/lance.glb`,
  `models/lance_faces.png` (drawn by `assets/drafts/characters/portraits/lance-face.mjs`).
- `assets/drafts/characters/portraits/`: the three sheets (`src/`), `png.mjs`,
  `sample.mjs`, `clean.mjs`, `preview.mjs`, `cast.mjs`, and the cleaned `<name>.px.js`.
- `docs/godot-art-style.md`: the new "Cutscene characters (3D)" section and both shaders.

## Left unfinished / known problems

- No rig or animation yet: he stands. The plan is a skeleton played stepped at about
  12 fps, like hand-drawn frames.
- His proportions are about 6.5 heads; the world's sprites are chibi (much bigger heads).
  Decide whether the 3D cast should match the sprites or stay taller.
- Pat and Ysha are not modelled yet. The cleaned portraits of Lance and Ysha still want a
  hand pass (Lance's tie and collar, Ysha's neck and lanyard).

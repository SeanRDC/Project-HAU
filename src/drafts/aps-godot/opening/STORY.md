# Project HAU: story draft

The story the opening sets up. Draft, kept in step with the team's "Project HAU: Story
Draft" doc. The opening itself, page by page, is in [SCRIPT.md](SCRIPT.md).

**Trigger warning:** this story deals with depression, suicide, grief and guilt. Nothing is
shown on screen, but the subject is central.

## What changed in this revision (2026-10-27)

Aim: emotional the way Omori is: by holding back, not by saying everything. The structure
stays; these parts changed. Disagree with any of them and say so.

1. **One date, not five coincidences.** November 11 is still the day Ysha dies and, ten
   years later, his child's birthday. It is no longer also her birthday, and 11:11 is used
   once (the call), not on clocks and jeep plates. Stacked coincidences read as fate, and
   fate reads as cheesy.
2. **Ysha is a person first.** New section "Who Ysha is": her humour, opinions, food, her bad
   drawings, a running joke with Lance. The table of times she reached out is now marked as
   a design tool: in play most of those moments look like nothing the first time.
3. **Fewer speeches.** Lines that said the theme out loud are cut or made ordinary: Pat's
   speech in ending 3 (she now hands back the photo and says one small thing), the mother's
   and the relative's lines (the pressure is shown, not said), and the true ending is renamed
   from "Hindi ka nag-iisa" to "Sabay tayo", her own words from June.
4. **Silence instead of the song.** "Sa likod" no longer has "Totoong Tayo" over a montage of
   Pat's future. She sets one drink on the ledge and walks away; the camera stays on the
   drink. (So the song permission is no longer needed.)
5. **Pat has edges.** She is hurt and sometimes unfair: a cold reply, a real fight, one
   jealous moment she is ashamed of. Her choice to help means more because of it.
6. **The cost is honesty, not romance.** The heart of the story is no longer "every hour for
   Ysha is taken from Pat" but "every secret he keeps to save Ysha is a lie he tells Pat".
   Lance and Ysha are friends; the ending where he stays is bittersweet, not love saving
   her.
7. **How it should feel:** long light stretches first, small wrongness used rarely, and the
   truth arriving as something the player does.
8. **His son or her** (like the choice in Shuzo Oshimi's *Drifting Net Cafe*). The child is
   a son, and Lance feels him even in 2026: in dreams, in the photo, in the word "Papa".
   Ysha is the first love he never told. The last night is the choice between them.
9. **The 2038 he goes back to is the one he left.** Going back returns him to his own
   future exactly as it was; the 2026 he changed carries on without him. So Ysha lives only
   in the ending where he stays. In every other ending she is gone in his world.
10. **Ending 3 is bittersweet, like *ID:INVADED* episode 10.** He saved her, she calls at
    11:11 PM (the call he once declined), he answers, tells her that where he comes from
    she is gone, and says he has to go back. "Saved, but alone" is dropped: three endings.
11. **What happened on November 11, like Mari in Omori.** Everyone, Lance most of all,
    believes Ysha gave up. The truth, found late: she had dengue nobody noticed behind her
    "pagod", she waited at the fountain in the rain because he promised to meet her at 9,
    she had a message to the guidance office in her drafts, and at 11:11 PM she called to
    ask him to take her to the ER and couldn't say it. Her depression stays real: it is why
    nobody saw she was sick.

## The story in short

A father full of regrets falls asleep in his old classroom at HAU and wakes up in June of
his third year, five months before November 11, the day he lost Ysha, the first love he
never told: everyone believed she gave up, after months of a sadness nobody noticed. He has one more chance, with only his memories
and one photo from his future: his wife Pat, his childhood friend, holding their newborn
son. To stay close to Ysha he starts lying to Pat. And the closer November comes, the
clearer the price: the world where Ysha lives is a world his son was never born into.

- **The heart of it:** a second chance, a secret he can't tell, a friend who notices anyway,
  and in the end a choice no one should have to make: his son or her.
- **Tone:** quiet, nostalgic, warm and funny for a long time, then sad, then bittersweet;
  told like the manga opening.
- **Language:** Tagalog and Taglish, with a little Kapampangan.
- **The game's question:** if he saves her, can he still go home?

Lance, Pat and Ysha are about 20, third-year students at HAU in 2026. Their son (in 2038,
two years old) is still to be named.

## Who Ysha is

Before she is anyone's worry, she has to be someone the player likes spending time with.
None of this is about her pain. (Drafts; the team should make her their own.)

- **Funny first.** Deadpan, quick, teases people she likes. Rates everything out of ten
  ("Yung adobo today? 4/10. Malungkot 'yung adobo.").
- **She draws, badly and proudly.** Manga in the margins of every handout: HAU as a fantasy
  kingdom, the guard as a knight, the canteen as a dungeon. The hands are terrible and she
  knows it. "Hindi pa ako marunong mag-drawing ng kamay. Next sem."
- **Opinions she will fight about.** Sisig is better at the Coop than outside; the fifth
  floor stairs are a human rights issue; pineapple on pizza is fine and everyone is wrong.
- **A running bit with Lance.** His notes are unreadable; she "translates" them out loud in
  a dramatic voice. It starts as a joke and becomes how they talk.
- **Small habits.** Always out of load; borrows a pen, keeps it. Knows every jeep driver on
  the Angeles route by name. Buys one milk tea and makes it last three classes.
- **What she wants.** To draw a real manga one day, about HAU. To get her family a house
  with a second floor. To pass Ethics, just once, without retaking it.

The player should miss these things before they understand why they stopped. When her
jokes get shorter, the margins of her handouts go blank, and the milk tea stops coming, no
one says anything; the player just notices it is quiet.

## The rules

- **It isn't time travel; he is pulled into his past life.** He falls asleep in the empty
  classroom in 2038 and wakes up living his own life at 20, in June 2026, with all his
  adult memories. Nobody else knows.
- **Only his memories, and the photo.** There are no notes. The only thing that comes back
  with him is his wallet, and in it the family photo. In the game his memories are the
  quest log.
- **The family photo.** In the hospital on November 11, 2036, he took a selfie: him beside
  Pat in the bed, their newborn son in her arms. Weeks later she printed it, wrote his
  name and the date on the back, signed it, and slipped it into his wallet without a word.
  She has no idea what it will do. Their son was born on the day he lost Ysha, ten years
  later.
- **November 11 is never shown.** Only the date and what it left behind.
- **Choices are remembered,** through the NPC memory the game already has.
- **The 2038 he goes back to is the one he left.** Falling asleep in the classroom on
  November 11 returns him to his own future, exactly as he left it: his son, his wife, and
  Ysha gone. The 2026 he changed carries on without him, and he can never know how. Nothing
  he does in 2026 reaches the 2038 he wakes in. Only staying keeps the world he changed.
- **The photo fades.** It shows the future he is about to lose. The more he lies to Pat or
  shuts her out, the more it changes: first Pat's face blurs, then his son. Being honest
  with Pat brings them back. The player can check it from the menu. It is a warning, not a
  cause: in 2026, Pat drifting away is real.

## How he is sent back

Nobody sends him on purpose, and nobody ever explains it.

1. **November 11, 2038.** Their child turns two. Alone in the car outside the party, he
   finally plays Ysha's voicemail from twelve years ago. Then he drives to HAU instead of
   going home, for the first time in years.
2. **The classroom.** Empty for the long weekend. His old third-year classroom, the window
   seat, the same afternoon light.
3. **The photo.** He looks at it: the best day of his life, on the date of the worst.
4. **"Sana..."** He falls asleep at the desk.
5. **"Huy. Gising na. Late ka na naman."** He wakes in June 2026 in the same seat, Pat
   shaking him awake. The wallet is in his pocket, the photo still inside.

**The way back.** On November 11, 2026 he feels the same pull to the same seat. If he falls
asleep there, he wakes in 2038 (endings 2 and 3). If he refuses, he stays in 2026 (ending 1).

## His son, or her

The dilemma that grows under everything (like the choice in Shuzo Oshimi's *Drifting Net
Cafe*). It is never said out loud until the last night; the player feels it first.

- **His son is there in 2026, in pieces.** When Lance dozes off (in class, on the jeep), a
  second of 2038 leaks in: a small hand on his face, "Papa", the smell of baby powder. The
  player can't control when. They come more often as November gets closer.
- **The photo is his son's face.** Checking it from the menu is the only way Lance can see
  him. When the photo fades, those dream-moments fade too.
- **He realises the price slowly.** In October he understands the rule: if he goes back, he
  wakes in the world he left, where she is gone. If he stays, his son will never be born.
  Nobody explains it to him; he works it out from the photo, and so does the player.
- **The last night is the choice.** November 11, 2026, the classroom, the window seat. The
  photo in one hand, the phone in the other. Sleep, and go home to his son. Stay awake, and
  stay in the world where she lives.

## The ending's call

Ending 3, like *ID:INVADED* episode 10, where the detective says goodbye to his daughter in
the world where she is alive, knowing that in the real one she is gone.

At 9 PM he was at the fountain, as promised. He saw her shivering and took her to the ER;
Pat came with them. Now it is late, she is admitted and safe, and he is alone in the
classroom, feeling the pull. At 11:11 PM Ysha calls, from a hospital bed. It is the same
call, at the same minute, that the player had to decline at the start of the game. This
time the only button is "Answer". She is fine: sleepy, teasing, an IV in her arm. "Uy. Gising ka pa? Wala lang... Salamat pala kanina. Sabay tayo
bukas, ha?" He lets her talk. Then he tells her the truth, plainly: "Ysha... sa pinanggalingan
ko, wala ka na. Kailangan kong bumalik." She doesn't understand. She laughs, the way she does:
"Ang weird mo. Matulog ka na nga." And then, completely her: "Ingat ka, Lance. Bukas." He stays
on the line after she hangs up, then puts his head down on the desk.

His wife never knows where he went. To her it was just an afternoon he came home late.

## Pat's story

- **Growing up.** The same street, the same schools, the same jeep home. She has loved him
  for years and never said it.
- **June.** He is different: older, quieter, and always somewhere else. He lies to her about
  a group project, badly. She knows. She says nothing yet.
- **July: the cold week.** She stops waiting outside his classroom for a few days, without
  explaining. When he asks, "Busy ako." It is the first time in their lives she doesn't wait.
- **August: the photo.** He drops his wallet in the canteen. The photo falls out: a hospital
  room, him older, and her, holding a baby. On the back, her own signature, only older, the
  baby's name, and "November 11, 2036". She puts it back before he sees. She doesn't
  understand it, and she can't stop thinking about it.
- **The fight.** Before she chooses anything, she says the unfair thing: about Ysha, in front
  of people, and regrets it the second it is out. She doesn't apologise for days.
- **The choice.** Then, quietly, she decides not to hold him back, and starts looking after
  Ysha too, without telling him: a seat saved, a text, a second milk tea.
- **Sa likod (September).** She sees them under the walkway, his hand on Ysha's shoulder,
  Ysha crying for the first time. Pat has two drinks. She sets one on the fountain ledge and
  walks away. The camera stays on the drink. No music.
- **The true ending.** She gives back the photo, turned to the signature, and says only: "Ito
  'yung nahulog mo noon." Nothing else. He understands.

## Ysha's story

No single terrible thing happened to Ysha. A lot of ordinary heavy things piled up,
quietly, and nobody was close enough to see the pile.

- **The eldest, and the bright one.** Panganay, the first to go to college. At home there is
  an empty space on the wall next to her parents' wedding photo, kept for her diploma.
  Nobody has to say anything about it.
- **First year:** a scholarship, a block of friends, good grades, a sketchbook full.
- **Second year:** her father skips work to drive her to an exam in a storm, and is fired
  for it. Nobody blames her; she blames herself. Her mother's health gets worse. She works
  nights at a milk tea shop and sleeps four hours a night.
- **She falls behind,** loses the scholarship, and her block moves on without her. She
  becomes irregular. At home she says school is fine; at school she says home is fine.
- **Third year (2026):** at first, the player only sees the funny one. Then, slowly: she
  stops eating lunch, the margins of her handouts go blank, she replies "haha" to
  everything, she stops making plans past next week.

### The weight she carried in 2026

1. **Her father's job.** Never discussed. She checks job posts on her phone in class, for
   him.
2. **Lola (July).** The only one who still asked "Kumain ka na ba, apo?" dies. After that,
   nobody asks. She plays her lola's last voice message on the jeep home.
3. **The rumour (September).** A joke about "the irreg girl who failed" (it started with
   Lance) in a big HAU group chat. She replies "haha grabe kayo". In the game the rumour
   spreads through the NPCs.
4. **The letter (November 10).** She will be dropped at the end of the semester. She tells
   no one. That night: "Lance, may sasabihin sana ako bukas. Okay lang ba?"

In the true ending none of these is fixed by Lance; she just doesn't carry them alone.

## What lies underneath (the reveals)

Truths hidden from the player, and from Lance, until late.

- **The rumour started with Lance.** In August Ysha tells only him she is failing again and
  makes him promise not to tell. Weeks later he jokes with Pat and friends: "Si Ysha nga,
  irreg na, babagsak pa ulit." Someone posts it. Late in the game the player traces the
  rumour back through the NPCs' memories themselves, person by person, and the trail ends
  at him. Nobody tells the player; they find it.
- **She chose him,** because he was the only one who lent her notes without asking anything
  back. To him it was nothing; to her it was the one kindness left.
- **Memories change when revisited.** The bright early 2026 is how Lance wants to remember
  it. Later the same scenes replay with what he edited out: her empty tray, her eyes on the
  jeep window, a joke that comes a second too late, the HAU letter in her bag.
- **The opening was her sketchbook.** Late in the game Lance finds her sketchbook of HAU and
  recognises every page of the opening, bad hands and all. The pages thin out from June to
  November; the last ones are blank.
- **Her doodle self.** She draws herself as a faceless stick figure. Two or three times in
  the whole game, when she says "okay lang ako", her sprite flickers into it for one frame.
  Rare enough that players wonder if they saw it.
- **The mother who smiles.** Kind, tired, proud, and always busy. "Anak, kumain ka na? Sige,
  galingan mo ha." Never the wrong words; just never the question.
- **Her hidden account,** drawings and real thoughts at 3 AM under another name. In the true
  ending she shows it to Lance and Pat; otherwise Lance finds it in 2038.
- **Wordless pages:** scenes without words, her face in close-up, smiling a little too long.

None of these is "the cause": her pain stays layered.

## What happened on November 11, 2026

**Trigger warning:** depression, suicide (what everyone believes), illness, guilt. Nothing
is shown.

The truth Lance has buried for twelve years; the player learns it late, in fragments. Like
Mari in Omori: what everyone believes is not what happened, and the hero is tied to it.

1. **A week before.** Ysha is tired: more than usual. Her head aches, her body hurts, she is
   cold in a warm room. It is dengue (November, after the typhoons), but she tells no one,
   because she is always tired, and "pagod" is the one thing nobody questions about her. At
   the canteen she pushes her tray away. Lance notices and thinks: again. He doesn't ask.
2. **November 10.** To her mother: "Ma, pagod na pagod na ako." "Magpahinga ka lang, anak.
   Konting tiis na lang." To Lance: "Lance, may sasabihin sana ako bukas. Okay lang ba?"
   Seen. No reply.
3. **The afternoon.** By the stairs, her voice small: "Lance... pwede ba tayong mag-usap
   mamaya?" He is tired and it comes out wrong: "Ikaw na naman." Then, guilty: "Sige,
   mamaya. Sa fountain, 9. Promise." She smiles, really smiles, for the first time in weeks.
   "Promise ha."
4. **The evening.** Pat asks him to eat with her after class. He forgets the fountain. It
   becomes their first date. He laughs more that night than he has all year.
5. **9:00 PM.** Ysha is at the fountain, early. A drizzle. She feeds the campus cat the bread
   she saved from the lunch she didn't eat. "Nandito na ako."
6. **10:00 PM.** Heavier rain, a worse fever; she is shivering. She stays on the ledge,
   because he promised. "Okay lang, baka busy ka. Hintayin kita konti pa."
7. **10:40 PM.** She types to the HAU guidance office: "Pwede po ba akong magpa-schedule
   bukas? Hindi na po kasi ako—" It stays in her drafts.
8. **11:11 PM.** She calls him. She meant to say "Lance, hindi ako okay. Pwede mo ba akong
   samahan sa ER?" When it goes to voicemail she can't, she never could, so she says the
   ordinary thing:

   > "Uy, Lance. 'Yung notes mo sa Ethics, nasa akin pa pala. Ibabalik ko na lang bukas.
   > ...Sige."

   Across town Lance sees her name and declines. "Bukas na lang," to no one. Pat asks who
   it was. "Wala."
9. **After.** She walks home in the rain, slowly. She lies down because she is tired, the
   way she is always tired.
10. **The next morning,** her seat is empty. That is all the story ever shows.
11. **The lie.** He says he hadn't talked to her that day and deletes the call and her
    texts. Pat never learns. Their whole life together began on the night Ysha waited.
12. **The birthday he can't celebrate.** His son is born on November 11, ten years later.

**What everyone believes, Lance most of all:** they all saw the signs (the skipped lunches,
the "haha", the blank margins), so they believe she gave up. For twelve years Lance lives
with it: she reached out, he didn't answer, and she ended it.

**The truth, found late:**

- **The hospital record:** severe dengue. She didn't give up; she was sick, and nobody
  noticed, because everyone, Lance most of all, had long ago decided that her being tired
  was just who she was. "Pagod na pagod na ako" meant both.
- **The unsent draft:** "Pwede po ba akong magpa-schedule bukas?" She was reaching for
  help. She was planning tomorrow.
- **The voicemail** means exactly what it says. "Ibabalik ko na lang bukas." She believed in
  bukas; he is the one who gave up on it, every November 11, for twelve years.
- **Her two texts from the fountain,** recovered at last: "Nandito na ako." "Hintayin kita
  konti pa."

The truth doesn't free him. It is worse, and gentler, at once: she wasn't giving up; she was
waiting for him.

### How the trauma is shown

The weight comes from memory, aftermath and guilt, never from showing how she died.

- **The memory distorts, rarely:** the classroom stretches, a lamp flickers, her voice
  repeats one word wrong. Used a handful of times in the whole game, so each one lands.
- **The aftermath:** her bag on the chair for a week; the group chat asking "Ysha, nasaan
  ka?"; her mother folding her uniform at the wake; her sibling: "Kuya, kaibigan ka ba ni
  Ate?"; her phone, with one call in the log that never connected; his Ethics notes, handed
  back by her mother, her translations of his handwriting in every margin and on the last
  page: "Salamat ulit. Sa susunod, ako naman manlilibre."; the campus cat, waiting at the
  fountain at 9 PM every night for bread that doesn't come.
- **The player is complicit:** early on, her call comes and the only button is "Decline".
- **The guilt rots him:** he can't hold his child on November 11; he flinches at phones at
  night.
- **The death stays unseen:** one black screen, a phone buzzing on a desk. Then morning.

### What the story must still say

No single person, and no single call, decides someone's life. Lance is responsible for
answering this time, not for everything before; someone says it to his face, once, and
plainly. Every release opens with a content warning and ends with help information (in the
Philippines, the NCMH crisis line 1553; check current numbers before release).

## How the story should feel

Love ordinary life, lose it, and get it back only through many small acts of noticing. Like
Omori: hold back, and trust the player.

1. **Long light stretches first.** June and July should be fun to play for their own sake,
   not foreshadowing: shared notes, the jeep after night class, the canteen table, Ysha
   translating his handwriting, Pat dragging Lance to class. When those routines stop, the
   player feels it before anyone says a word.
2. **A playable prologue in 2038.** Before the opening, one short day as Lance on November
   11, 2038: feeding their two-year-old, Pat teasing him, the birthday cake, a game on the
   floor, his son calling him "Papa"; then the car and the voicemail. The family in the
   photo becomes a child the player has held, so the last night's choice truly costs
   something either way.
3. **Show, don't say.** No one names the theme. Feelings come through objects and habits: a
   drink on a ledge, a blank margin, a pen that stops being returned, a seat nobody saves.
4. **Small wrongness, rarely.** The doodle flicker and the distortions are rare, so the
   player is never sure what they saw.
5. **The truth is something the player does.** Tracing the rumour, finding the sketchbook,
   playing the voicemail: the player uncovers it, nobody explains it.
6. **Small kindnesses add up to the true ending.** Each of Ysha's reach-outs the player
   catches restores a little of the photo. Attentive play earns ending 3.
7. **Let the grief take time.** Where she is lost, show the empty months before 2038: Lance
   not going to class, her seat, the rain, the silent group chat, Pat knocking and him not
   opening the door.

- **Ending 1: Ang Pinili Ko.** He leaves the photo on the classroom desk and stays awake
  until morning. He stays. Ysha gets help, slowly, and has a life that isn't about him; they
  stay friends, and he never tells her why he was always there. Pat finds the photo and
  watches it fade in her hand, her own face, then their son, until only he is left. Years later
  he is happy, mostly. But every November 11 he wakes at night sure he heard "Papa", and
  the last panel is his wallet with an empty slot.
- **Ending 2: The same November 11.** He keeps Ysha at a distance, thinking there is time,
  and misses her signs again. At 11:11 PM on November 11, 2026 he is out with Pat, his phone
  on silent in his bag. The player sees the screen light up, "Ysha", and stop before anyone
  notices: the same missed call, a second time. She is gone in this world too. That night
  in the classroom there is nothing left to choose; he sleeps and wakes in the 2038 he left,
  having lost her twice. At the birthday his wife (who still knows nothing) finds him in
  the car. She doesn't ask. She sits with him. The last shot is the empty classroom.
- **Ending 3: Sabay tayo.** In October he tells Pat everything. She is quiet for a long
  time, then: "Sige." They sit with Ysha, walk her to the jeep, go with her to the guidance
  office and to her family. On the afternoon of November 11 she is with them, under one
  umbrella too small for three. At the fountain Pat hands back the photo, signature side up:
  "Ito 'yung nahulog mo noon." At 9 PM he keeps the promise, sees she is sick, and they
  take her to the ER. That night, in the classroom, comes the call ("The ending's
  call" above): he answers, tells Ysha that where he comes from she is gone, and that he has
  to go back. He sleeps. 2038: the car outside the party. In his world she is still gone,
  and nothing he did changed that. But this time he goes back inside and tells his wife
  everything, and she believes him. His son runs to him: "Papa!" The last shot is the
  birthday table, with an empty chair he leaves for Ysha on purpose, and on the
  classroom desk in 2026, a sketch of the three of them, the hands still terrible.

Ysha's recovery is never "fixed by love": where she lives, it is because she got real help
and people stayed. Where she is gone, it is because he went home, not because help failed.
